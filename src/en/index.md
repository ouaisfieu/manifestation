## Practical information {:#practical}

- **What**: a national demonstration called jointly by the CSC/ACV, FGTB/ABVV and Synova unions and covered by strike notices. **It is not a general strike.**{% s "csc-appel", "fgtb-appel", "synova-appel" %}
- **Where and when**: gather from 10:00 on Boulevard Roi Albert II (Brussels-North station). The march sets off around 11:00 via Boulevard du Jardin Botanique and the inner ring road and ends around 13:00 at Esplanade de l'Europe (Brussels-South station){% s "rtbf-police", "lalibre-mobilisation" %}.
- **Disruption**: heavy disruption on STIB (Brussels metro, tram, bus), reduced De Lijn and TEC services. **No rail strike (SNCB)**, but very crowded trains. Road and tunnel closures in Brussels from 09:00 to 15:00{% s "bruzz-mivb", "rtbf-transports", "bt-traffic" %}.

## Why 23 November? {:#stakes}

The five-party "Arizona" coalition (N-VA, MR, Les Engagés, CD&V, Vooruit) is looking for roughly €10 billion by 2029{% s "afp-sursis" %}. On 6 November the King refused De Wever's resignation. On 15 November four parties backed a new proposal, but Vooruit did not, and the Prime Minister was given until **23 November**, the day of the march, to report back{% s "bruzz-roi", "bx1-roi" %}. The dispute is less about whether savings are needed than about **who bears them**, which is exactly the fault line inside the coalition.

## Demands and counter-arguments {:#demands}

- **Unions**: no changes to automatic wage indexation, public holidays or eco-vouchers, no VAT increase, no annualised working time. They put forward a tax "Plan B" said to raise €21.2 billion a year (FGTB's own costing, not independently assessed){% s "fgtb-planb", "lavenir-audition" %}.
- **Employers**: Flemish employers' group Voka calls it "a new demonstration without a serious plan". FEB/VBO points to rising spending and calls for a growth plan. Voka and Unizo campaign against new taxes on entrepreneurship{% s "voka-betoging", "feb-tango", "voka-unizo-actie" %}.

## Media framing {:#media}

Local and practical outlets focus on disruption (tunnels, metro, waste collection). Political reporting focuses on cabinet arithmetic, employer and HR outlets on absenteeism and economic risk, and alternative media on tax justice. Several English-language and expat outlets wrongly describe the day as a "national strike"{% s "bt-strike", "deeparrival" %}. French-language outlets tend to echo the organisers' vocabulary, while Dutch-language outlets put more emphasis on the rioting of 9 October and on employers' criticism.
